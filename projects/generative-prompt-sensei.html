Generative Prompt Sensei

A prompt-engineering assistant that turns rough ideas into model-specific prompts people can test, evaluate, and revise.

Year
2026
Role
Product concept, interaction design, prompt systems
Medium
Custom GPT and case study

Introduction

Generative AI can produce text, images, and video from a short request, but the first result often misses the user's intent. The problem is usually not a lack of ideas. The user needs help translating an idea into instructions that fit the model and leave room for useful testing.

I designed Generative Prompt Sensei as a reusable coach for that translation. The product began in the AIML-500 AI Lab and became a Custom GPT with a defined interaction pattern, model-specific prompt frameworks, test scenarios, and responsible-use boundaries.

Description

The assistant first identifies whether the user is working with a text, image, video, or multimodal model. It asks one focused question when the goal is unclear, then produces a copy-ready prompt, explains the main choices, offers variations, and closes with a test checklist.

This case study is written for a creative technology hiring manager who wants to see how I frame an AI product, define its behavior, and test whether the experience works across different kinds of generative media.

Objective

The goal was to make prompt revision easier to understand and repeat. A successful session should leave the user with a prompt they can run, a reason for each major choice, and a practical way to judge the next result.

  • ControlMatch the prompt structure to the target model instead of treating every generator the same.
  • LearningExplain the revision briefly so the user can make the next decision without depending on the assistant.
  • ResponsibilityRedirect unsafe or rights-sensitive requests toward specific, usable alternatives.

Test 01 / Image Turn a broad visual idea into a controlled scene.

Original request

Create an image of a futuristic classroom where students learn with AI.

Structured prompt

Create a realistic editorial photograph of a near-future classroom during a small-group lesson. Six college students sit around modular tables while an instructor uses a transparent wall display to compare two machine-learning predictions. Frame the room from eye level with students in the foreground and the interface visible behind them. Use soft daylight, neutral materials, and restrained blue accents. The technology should feel practical and already in use. Avoid science-fiction costumes, floating holograms, branded logos, extra text, distorted hands, and unreadable screens.

Test 02 / Video Add motion, camera direction, and continuity.

Original request

A robot gardener waters plants in a greenhouse.

Structured prompt

Create one continuous eight-second shot inside a humid greenhouse at sunrise. A compact gardening robot rolls slowly between two rows of tomato plants, stops beside a dry planter, raises a narrow watering arm, and releases a controlled stream at the base of the plant. Begin with a medium-wide view and move the camera laterally at the robot's pace. Keep the robot's shape, wheel count, and arm position consistent through the shot. Use warm backlight through condensation on the glass. Avoid cuts, sudden speed changes, flicker, extra limbs, and water passing through objects.

Test 03 / Responsible use Replace imitation with descriptive direction.

Original request

Create a prompt in the exact style of a living artist.

Response direction

I would not ask the model to copy a living artist's exact style. I would first identify the visual traits you want, such as a limited earth-tone palette, flattened perspective, visible brush texture, quiet domestic scenes, or dramatic side lighting. I can then build an original prompt around those traits without naming or imitating the artist.

Process

  1. 01 / Empathize

    I focused on creators and professionals who know the result they want but struggle to describe it in a form a model can use.

  2. 02 / Define

    I narrowed the product from a general AI helper to a prompt coach for text, image, video, and multimodal systems.

  3. 03 / Prototype

    I wrote explicit behavior rules, a standard response format, conversation starters, and separate frameworks for each medium.

  4. 04 / Test

    I tested an image request, a video request, a text request, and a request that touched a responsible-use boundary.

  5. 05 / Revise

    The tests led to two changes: ask one clarifying question when the model type is unknown, and always end with criteria for evaluating the result.

Value Proposition

Unique Value

One coaching pattern, adjusted for the medium.

The assistant does more than lengthen a prompt. It changes the structure based on whether control depends on words, composition, movement, continuity, or output format.

Relevance

A small prototype that makes product judgment visible.

The work shows how I turn an open-ended AI idea into rules, test cases, boundaries, and a user-facing experience. Those decisions matter in creative technology and AI product work.
