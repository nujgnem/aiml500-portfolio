Artifact 1

From Symbolic Rules to Generative Systems: AI and ML Timeline

Introduction. This visual timeline traces the development of artificial intelligence and machine learning from early questions about machine intelligence to the current era of generative systems, frontier models, cloud infrastructure, and AI governance.

Description. The artifact uses a radial timeline to show major milestones, including Turing's imitation game, the Dartmouth workshop, perceptrons, ELIZA, AI winters, expert systems, Deep Blue, deep learning, AlexNet, AlphaGo, transformers, ChatGPT, and the 2024-2026 frontier model period.

Objective. The goal is to help beginning AI/ML learners see that AI progress does not come from one breakthrough alone. It usually emerges when research ideas, data, computing power, funding, practical use, and public trust move together.

Value proposition. The timeline turns a long technical history into a visual resource that can support class discussion, professional learning, and quick orientation for people new to AI/ML.

Unique value. The radial layout communicates connection across decades rather than presenting history as a simple straight line. It also highlights cycles of optimism, constraint, renewed methods, infrastructure growth, and social responsibility.

Relevance. The artifact is relevant to Machine Learning Fundamentals because it connects core AI/ML concepts with historical development, leading companies, technical breakthroughs, and ethical pressure around responsible adoption.

Research

I gathered major AI and ML milestones from course materials, credible historical sources, research papers, and industry references.

Organize

I grouped events into foundations, symbolic AI, AI winters, deep learning, generative AI, and the frontier model period.

Design

I created a radial layout so viewers could see both chronology and connection across technical eras.

Revise

I refined the milestone explanations, visual spacing, image choices, and disclosure language before using the artifact in discussion.

Tools: Research sources, CSV timeline data, HTML/CSS layout, image export, and AI-assisted drafting reviewed by the student.
Technologies: Web-based visual layout, radial composition, structured milestone data, and exported PNG/PDF evidence.
Audience: Beginning AI/ML learners, classmates, and professionals who need a concise view of AI history.
Artifact files: Timeline PNG, poster HTML, PDF export, data CSV, and final discussion post.
